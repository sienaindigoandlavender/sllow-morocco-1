8 centuries of lions in the palace

Amazigh chiefs took cubs in the Atlas and brought them to the sultan, and the practice ran unbroken from the Almohads in the twelfth century to the Alaouites in the twentieth. When the mountains emptied, the palace still had lions, and the whole surviving North African lineage traces back to that collection.

Updated 7 September 2026 Genetic work on the royal lineage is ongoing and its findings are given as they stand.

It hunted wild boar and Barbary deer in the Atlas, in country that looks more like southern Europe than anywhere south of the Sahara. Its range ran the length of North Africa, from Morocco to Egypt.

The collection

Lions passed from sultan to successor as part of the inheritance. When the royal family went into exile in 1953 the collection was dispersed, three to Casablanca and the rest to Meknes, and it was rebuilt afterwards.

The 1973 group was handed to Rabat Zoo, which still holds the largest single population. Others went to European institutions, where cubs have since been born at Neuwied, Pilsen, Hannover, Erfurt, Heidelberg, Olomouc and Port Lympne.

Where the bloodline sits now

A conservation population held in two places at once, with Rabat as the source and the European zoos as the insurance.

The record

Roman era
Taken for the arena
North African lions were shipped to Rome in quantity. The demand is part of why the population thinned so early.
1200s
In the Tower of London
Skulls recovered from the royal menagerie were genetically identified as pure-bred Panthera leo leo. Moroccan lions, in England, eight hundred years ago.
12th–20th c.
The palace collection
Cubs taken in the Atlas and presented to the sultan, from the Almohads through to the Alaouites.
19th c.
Firearms and bounties
Colonial administrations paid for lions shot. The forest was cleared at the same time.
1942
The last confirmed wild lion
Shot on the northern side of the Tizi n’Tichka, on the road between Marrakech and Ouarzazate. Algeria’s last was recorded in 1943.
1960s
Possibly later than that
Modelling published in 2013 concluded small groups may have persisted in Algeria into the early 1960s and in Morocco into the mid-1960s.
1973
To Rabat Zoo
The royal population, by then 88 animals, is transferred and a managed programme begins.
