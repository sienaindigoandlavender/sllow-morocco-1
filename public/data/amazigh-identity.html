From the Canaries to Siwa

Tamazgha is the Amazigh homeland and it does not fit inside any modern border. It runs from islands in the Atlantic to an oasis in western Egypt, across eleven countries, and the largest single population of it lives in Morocco.

Tamazgha Diaspora

Roughly 14 million people speak an Amazigh language. In Morocco, 40 to 45% of the population claims Amazigh heritage; in Algeria, 25 to 30%.

A script older than the Latin alphabet's arrival in Europe

The Numidians developed Tifinagh somewhere between the sixth and fourth centuries BCE, taking some letters from Phoenician and Punic and inventing the rest. The Tuareg kept it in continuous use while everyone else wrote Amazigh in Arabic or Latin characters.

The calendar nobody else uses

Yennayer falls on 14 January. The Amazigh year runs 950 ahead of the Gregorian one, so January 2026 opened the year 2976. It is a public holiday in Algeria, and Moroccan civic associations spent years petitioning for the same before recognition came.

It is also celebrated in Paris, Barcelona, Stockholm, Montreal and more than a dozen American cities, which is the clearest measure of how far the diaspora reaches.

One flag, three landscapes

Designed in 1970 by the Algerian activist Mohand Arav Bessaoud. The bands are the three terrains of Tamazgha and the letter at the centre is Yaz, the first letter of Amazigh, in red for the cost of holding onto it.
