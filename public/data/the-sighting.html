Nobody knows when Ramadan starts until somebody looks

Morocco does not publish the date in advance. On the twenty-ninth evening of Sha’ban, observers are sent out across the country, and if one of them sees the crescent the month begins at that sunset. If nobody sees it, Sha’ban runs a thirtieth day. The announcement comes the same night, on television, after dark.

Updated 8 September 2026 Astronomical figures are calculated. The determination is not.

Why it cannot simply be worked out

The moon and sun line up at conjunction, and from that instant the moon pulls away at roughly twelve degrees a day. The angle between them is called elongation, and it is what decides whether there is a crescent to see at all.

Below about seven degrees, no crescent is visible to any eye under any sky. That is the Danjon limit, and it is a physical floor rather than a matter of eyesight or weather. Between seven and roughly ten degrees the crescent exists and is generally too faint. Past twelve it can be caught, low in the west, for perhaps twenty minutes after the sun has gone.

Which means the moon can be technically new and completely unseeable, and the difference between those two states is a few hours.

The same moon, four sunsets

This is the part that explains the disagreements. Sunset moves westward around the earth. A city in the far west has its sunset hours later in absolute time than a city in the east, so by the time the sun goes down over Rabat the crescent has had several more hours to pull clear of it.

Morocco sits at the western edge of the Muslim world. That geography is an advantage on the first night and a liability on the last: the same extra hours that let Morocco see the Ramadan crescent early can, at the other end of the month, put its Eid a day behind the Gulf.

Observers are posted at points across the country and report in. Astronomical data tells the ministry where and when to look, and how plausible a sighting is. It does not substitute for one. A single credible witness can begin the month for everybody.

This is why Morocco frequently starts a day after Saudi Arabia and occasionally a day before its neighbours, and why none of those differences are disagreements about religion. They are four countries standing under the same sky at different hours.

The rule the month runs on

NightWhat happens
29 Sha’ban, crescent seenRamadan begins that evening. The first fast is the following dawn
29 Sha’ban, nothing seenSha’ban completes thirty days. Ramadan begins the next evening automatically, no sighting required
29 Ramadan, crescent seenEid al-Fitr the following day
29 Ramadan, nothing seenA thirtieth fast, then Eid

No month runs to thirty-one days and none is shorter than twenty-nine, because the lunar month is 29.53 days and the calendar simply rounds it by observation. Twelve of those make 354 days, eleven short of a solar year, which is why Ramadan walks backwards through the seasons.

2027

The conjunction falls on Saturday 6 February 2027 at 15:56 UTC. At sunset in Morocco that evening the crescent is around three hours old and far below the Danjon limit, so nothing will be seen. By sunset on Sunday 7 February it is roughly twenty-eight hours old and comfortably within range.

The likely first day of Ramadan 1448 in Morocco, following a sighting on the evening of Sunday 7 February. Eid al-Fitr falls around Tuesday 9 or Wednesday 10 March, depending on whether the month runs twenty-nine days or thirty — which nobody will know until the evening of the twenty-ninth.

That prediction is astronomy, and astronomy is reliable. It is still not the date. The date is what the ministry announces after somebody has stood outside and looked west.
