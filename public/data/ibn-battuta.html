11 km a day, for 11,000 days

Ibn Battuta left Tangier in 1325, aged twenty-one, to perform the Hajj. He came back twenty-nine years later having covered around 117,000 kilometres and reached the Volga, the coast of Tanzania, the ports of southern China and the court of Mali.

How far a man from Tangier got

The dashed lines are the outline, not the route. He doubled back constantly, crossed the same country several times, and the Rihla is not always in order. The four extremes are what matter: Bolghar on the Volga, Quanzhou in China, Kilwa on the Swahili coast, and Timbuktu on the far side of the Sahara.

A day, averaged across 11,000 days

That last figure is the one worth sitting with. 11 km is a morning’s walk. He did it every day for three decades, on foot, on camels and in ships, and it added up to three times round the earth.

Against the traveller everyone has heard of

Ibn Battuta, from Tangier
~117,000 km
1325 to 1354. Reached China sixty years after Polo, and went considerably further in every other direction. Wrote about people as much as places: a judge, a guest, a husband several times over, and an unusually good ethnographer.
Marco Polo, from Venice
~half that
A generation earlier, and known to every schoolchild in Europe. The comparison is not a competition so much as a question about which library a book ends up in.

He was a judge, which is why he could go

Ibn Battuta trained as a qadi in the Maliki school, and a qualified Islamic jurist was employable anywhere in Dar al-Islam. He served on the bench in Delhi and again in the Maldives. The legal training was the visa, the letter of credit and the job offer at once.

The book was dictated, from memory

He kept no journal. On his return the Marinid sultan Abu Inan Faris put a scribe at his disposal, Ibn Juzayy, and the Rihla was composed between 1352 and 1355 out of what Ibn Battuta could recall and what Ibn Juzayy chose to elaborate.

The Rihla dropped out of circulation for centuries and was recovered by European scholarship in the nineteenth. Morocco named 1996 and 1997 the Year of Ibn Battuta, and a museum opened in the Tangier kasbah in 2022.
