510 million years, and you can walk on all of it

Morocco is one of very few places where rock from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous is exposed at the surface within a day’s drive. Four separate worlds — a Cambrian sea, a Devonian reef, a Jurassic ocean and a Cretaceous river delta — stacked and then tipped up where anyone can reach them.

The column

The country sat under warm shallow sea for most of the Palaeozoic and became a river delta in the Cretaceous. Each of those environments left a layer, and the folding described in the Atlas geology brought them all back to the surface.

Where the beds are

The trade runs on a triangle: Alnif, Erfoud and Taouz. Alnif calls itself the trilobite capital of the world and sits within reach of Cambrian, Ordovician and Devonian rock at once. Jbel Isoumour is known simply as trilobite mountain.

That last figure is the part worth holding. The commercial diggers are also the discovery mechanism. Hundreds of taxa entered the scientific record because somebody quarrying for the shops found something nobody had seen.

The Kem Kem story starts the same way. In 1991 a shepherd at Hassi Begaa found fish bones. That led to the excavations that produced Spinosaurus aegyptiacus, the first dinosaur known to have been semiaquatic and the largest predator yet found.

How to read a specimen

Specimens vary from museum-grade to decorative, and the difference is readable once you know what to look at. Dealers in Erfoud and Alnif will talk you through it if you ask.

Signs of a real specimen
  • The fossil and the matrix are the same stone, with no seam between them
  • Asymmetry, breakage, missing spines. Real animals are damaged
  • Preparation marks under a lens: needle work, not casting texture
  • The species matches the bed it is claimed to come from
  • The seller can name the quarry
Signs of a decorative piece
  • Perfect symmetry, every spine intact, on a cheap piece
  • A colour or sheen that stops abruptly at the edge of the animal
  • Identical specimens in quantity on the same table
  • Glue lines, filler, or a body that sits on the rock rather than in it
  • Composites: a real head, a carved rest

Composites are the hardest to read, since part of the piece may be genuine and part of it assembled. A good dealer will tell you which is which.
