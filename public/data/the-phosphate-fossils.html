The fertiliser mine cuts through the day the dinosaurs died

Khouribga sells phosphate to farms in India and Brazil. The rock it sells is a 25-million-year marine sequence running from the Late Cretaceous into the Eocene, and somewhere in the working face is the boundary layer that marks the asteroid. The mine and one of the world’s great fossil beds are the same hole.

The face

Phosphate forms where biological matter settles in shallow sea and precipitates out of the water, so a phosphate bed is by definition full of what was living above it. Morocco sat under warm shallow ocean for the whole of that span. Every metre of ore is a compressed marine census.

Above the line and below it

Maastrichtian, before
Mosasaurs
The assemblage is dominated by mosasaurid squamates. Plesiosaurs, marine turtles in abundance, scarce dinosaurs, and pterosaurs.
Palaeogene, after
Crocodiles
Dyrosaurid and eusuchian crocodyliforms take the same ecological space. A mirrored fauna, doing the same job with different animals.

That substitution is the clearest thing the beds show. The sea did not empty; it was refilled from a different branch of the family tree, and both halves of the swap are in the same quarry.

What the pterosaurs settled

It was long argued that pterosaurs were already declining before the asteroid, squeezed out by birds, and that the extinction finished a job competition had started.

The Moroccan beds do not support that. At least seven pterosaur species from three families are present in the latest Cretaceous here, at large body sizes, right up to the boundary. They were diverse and common when the rock stops. Whatever ended them was sudden, and it was not birds.

Arambourg, and what changed after him

Camille Arambourg, 1885 to 1969, did the founding work on these basins and published thirteen reptile taxa from fragments. Since 1997 a joint French–Moroccan field programme has taken that to fifty-two, and shifted the material from isolated bones to articulated skeletons and skulls.

The mining is the reason. Continuous extraction keeps opening fresh face, which is a form of excavation nobody has to fund. The same operation that is removing the record is also the only thing exposing it.

One caution for buyers. Because mining breaks the rock, most specimens come out as isolated teeth. Otodus teeth are frequently sold set into matrix that was assembled in a workshop: the tooth is genuine, the rock around it is a display.
