195 by 160 metres, and it is a reservoir

The Menara basin is photographed as a mirror with the Atlas behind it. It was dug as water storage for a hundred hectares of olives, filled by underground channels from those mountains, and the pavilion was added four centuries later. The garden is the end of a machine.

The chain, mountain to tree

Snowmelt in the High Atlas, into a khettara, along a gallery cut at about a metre of fall per kilometre with shafts every so often for spoil and air, into the basin, and out through seguias to the plots. Nothing in it is pumped and nothing in it is powered. It is the same technology set out elsewhere in this archive, working at the scale of a capital city.

A working farm, laid out like an argument

Both sites are subdivided by paths into a patchwork of plots, and each plot carries one species. Olives here, oranges there. That is not decorative planting; it is crop management, and the rationalism of it is the signature of the Hispano-Mauresque productive garden.

The Agdal still runs orange orchards and olive groves fed by retention basins. The Menara olive grove is still harvested. Nine hundred years on, both are doing the job they were dug for, which is the part that gets left out of the photograph.

The road is the detail worth keeping. A sultan built himself a private route from his palace door to his orchard, and the orchard is still there while the palace, the gate and the road are not.

Two stories about the basin, and what to do with them

Guides tell you that Almohad soldiers trained in the basin, swimming lengths in preparation for crossing to al-Andalus. They also tell you that Moulay Ismail disposed of unwanted companions by dropping them from the pavilion balcony.

Neither is documented. The first is at least plausible in shape — an army that intended to campaign across water, and a large body of still water beside its capital. The second is a nineteenth-century sort of story about a seventeenth-century sultan, and the pavilion it is set in was not built until 1870. This archive repeats neither as fact.
