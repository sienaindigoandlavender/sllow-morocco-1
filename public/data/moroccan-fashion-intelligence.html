1 garment, 5 registers

The djellaba is not a costume. It is the ordinary outer garment of the country, and the same shape covers a man walking to the shop and a man walking to a wedding. What changes is the cloth, the trim, and how far it reaches the floor.

Updated 7 September 2026 Terminology and cut vary regionally. This is the general vocabulary.

The garment, and the ladder

Cut in one piece with wide sleeves, hooded, and trimmed down the front opening with sfifa: a braid worked in silk cord, with aqad buttons made from the same cord knotted into loops. The braid is where the money shows. Everything else about a djellaba is identical from top to bottom of the ladder.

The words, correctly

Djellaba
Hooded, loose, outdoor. Worn by men and women both, over other clothes
Kaftan
A woman’s formal robe, no hood, one piece. Ceremonial rather than outdoor
Takchita
Two layers: a plain under-dress and an open over-dress, closed with a mdamma belt
Selham
The wide sleeveless cape, worn over everything. Formal, and often white

The commonest confusion is kaftan and takchita. A kaftan is one garment; a takchita is two worn together and belted. If you can see two layers and a belt, it is a takchita.

AlsoWhat it is
Gandoura or tqashtaSleeveless or short-sleeved and unhooded. The summer version, worn at home
Babouche, or belghaThe backless leather slipper. Yellow is the formal colour for men
TarbouchThe felt cap. Fes gave its name to the red one in English
MdammaThe belt of a takchita, often the most expensive part of the whole outfit
HaikThe older white wrap, largely out of daily use and still seen in Chefchaouen and the north

What the cloth is doing

SeasonMaterial
Winter, and the mountainsHeavy wool, often undyed. A djellaba in Atlas wool is a coat, and the hood is a hood because it rains
SummerCotton and linen, pale, loose. The same silhouette moving air rather than trapping it
FormalSilk, brocade, and sfifa in metallic cord. Made to be seen under electric light at a wedding
The desertLoose, layered and light-coloured, and the head covered. Shade you carry with you

Loose is the whole principle. A garment held off the skin lets air move between cloth and body, and that moving air is what does the cooling. Tight light clothing is hotter than loose heavy clothing in this climate, which is counterintuitive until you have stood in both.

Regional tells

WhereWhat you see
Fes and TetouanThe finest sfifa work and the most restrained colour. An Andalusi inheritance
MarrakechBrighter, and more of it. The city has never been shy
The RifThe mendil: a red and white striped woven wrap, worn as a skirt and over the shoulders, with a wide straw hat
The Souss and the Anti-AtlasIndigo, and the silver described elsewhere in this archive. The jewellery is the outfit
The SaharaThe blue or white darraa, very wide, worn over trousers, with a long head cloth

What people actually wear

Most Moroccans dress like most people anywhere: jeans, shirts, trainers, for most of the week. The djellaba is what goes on over that for Friday prayer, for a visit, for a funeral, for the cold, and for anything that matters.

Which is why the ladder above is the useful way to read it. Nobody is in costume. They are dressed at a particular level of formality, and the level is legible at a glance to everybody except the visitor.
