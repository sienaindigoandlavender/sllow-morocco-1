Two thousand years, then twenty

Jews lived in Morocco for longer than Islam has existed, and it held the largest Jewish community in the Muslim world. Most of it left inside a single generation.

Where the mellahs were

Every city of any size had one. The word comes from the Arabic for salt. Fes built the first in 1438; the rest followed over the next four centuries, and the communities of the south were Amazigh-speaking and much older than the Sephardi families of the north.

The population, and then the departure

Estimates for the 1948 peak run from 250,000 to 270,000. Operation Yachin moved roughly 90,000 people to Israel between 1961 and 1964 alone. By 1971 there were 25,000 left.

The community is now somewhere else

Morocco still has the largest Jewish community in the Arab world. It is also a rounding error against the number of Moroccan Jews living outside it.

Others went to France, Canada, the United States and South America. Roughly four hundred Moroccan Jews now live outside Morocco for every one inside it.

Two roots, not one

Treating "Moroccan Jewish" as a single thing loses the more interesting half of the story. The two communities produced different liturgies, different cooking, different names and different dialects.

ToshavimIndigenous, present from antiquity. Amazigh-speaking in the interior and the south. Possibly arrived with Phoenician traders.

The 2011 constitution recognises the Hebraic component of Moroccan identity, and the state has funded cemetery and synagogue restoration since 2010. The hiloula pilgrimages still bring diaspora families back each year, which is the one direction of travel that has never stopped.
