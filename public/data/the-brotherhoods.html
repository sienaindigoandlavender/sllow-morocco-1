An order founded in Fes reaches 20 countries

Ahmad al-Tijani founded his tariqa in Fes around 1781. It is now the largest Sufi movement in West Africa, its followers number in the tens of millions at least, and every year they come back to the city where it started, because that is where he is buried.

The reach

Senegal, Ghana, Nigeria, Mauritania, Mali, Côte d’Ivoire, Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad, Sudan and beyond. Tijani clerics run Quranic schools, community centres and retreats, and in Senegal, Mali and parts of Nigeria they sit in mediation, education and political advisory roles.

The routes are not a coincidence. They are the caravan roads, described elsewhere in this archive, running in a later century with a different cargo.

The 300 million figure is the order’s own and has been reported rather than verified. No independent census of tariqa membership exists anywhere, and in Morocco many people are culturally Sufi without any formal affiliation, which makes counting close to meaningless. What is not in doubt is that the Tijaniyya is the largest Sufi movement in West Africa.

How an order is structured

Shaykh
The guide, at the top, often from a hereditary line. The authority is spiritual rather than administrative, and it does not transfer by election.
Muqaddam
Deputies who run local branches. Plural muqaddamin. In practice they are the order: they initiate, teach and hold a place together.
Murid
The disciple, who pledges allegiance and undertakes regular dhikr. The word means one who desires.
Silsila
The chain of transmission back through named teachers. The Boutchichiyya trace theirs to Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani of Baghdad, which is what people in Niger cite as the reason for joining it.
Zaouia
The building and the community in it. The word means corner. Historically a lodge, a school, a hostel, a court of arbitration and a food bank at once.

From heritage to policy

Sufism in Morocco moved from private practice to a stated element of state religious policy over about two decades, and the dates are on the record.

DateWhat happened
November 2002Ahmed Toufiq, historian and a Boutchichi disciple, is appointed Minister of Islamic Affairs, succeeding a minister of eighteen years
After 2003Following the Casablanca bombings, Sufism is publicly promoted as part of the state’s religious framework, alongside the Maliki school and Ash’ari theology
2011The King addresses the third international forum of Tijaniyya followers, held in Fes
OngoingThe Mohammed VI Institute for the Training of Imams in Rabat trains African and European imams. The Mohammed VI Foundation of African Ulema operates across the continent
December 2024The 44th Islamic Cultural Days are held in Dakar under royal patronage, with Tijani scholars and the Senegalese president present

The moussem

The saint festivals are where the orders meet the general population. Millions attend moussems, Mawlid celebrations and shrine visits every year, most of them people who belong to no tariqa at all. The Darqawiya moussem runs for one day on the last Sunday of September; the Fes Festival of Sufi Culture draws audiences from as far as South America.
