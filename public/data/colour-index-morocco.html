4 of these are minerals, 3 are alive

Moroccan colour is usually described as a mood. It is mostly chemistry: iron in the earth, copper in a glaze, cobalt that survives a kiln, an insect on a cactus, a leaf that binds to keratin. Each colour is where it is because of what was available there.

The board

Why Marrakech is that colour

The red is not paint. It is the earth the city is built from: local soil high in iron oxide, rammed into walls and rendered with more of the same. A pisé wall in the Haouz is that colour before anybody decides anything.

What was decided is that everything else should match. A municipal ruling in the 1960s required new building in the city to use the same range, which is why a 1970s apartment block and a twelfth-century gate are the same hue. The colour is geological; the consistency is administrative.

The blues are four different substances

Indigo
A plant dye, traded north across the Sahara. Rubs off, which is the point in the desert
Cobalt
A mineral that survives kiln temperature. The only reason zellige can be blue
Lime wash
Pigment in a lime carrier. Cheap, breathable, and repainted every year
Ultramarine
Once ground lapis, now synthetic. Jacques Majorelle’s garden, from 1937

Which is why they behave so differently. Chefchaouen needs repainting and Fes pottery does not. A Tuareg tagelmust stains the skin and a zellige tile is glass. Four blues, four chemistries, four different jobs.

Saffron sits between the categories. It is a pigment, a dye and a spice from the same three stigmas, priced by weight against metals, and used for all three purposes in the same kitchen.

Tadelakt is a technique, not a colour

Marrakech lime plaster, pigmented, applied in thin coats and then compressed with a hard stone until the surface closes. Sealed afterwards with black soap, which reacts with the lime to make it water-resistant.

It takes any pigment, so tadelakt grey is simply the version with none added. The colour people associate with it is the colour of the lime itself, which comes out of the quarries north of the city.
