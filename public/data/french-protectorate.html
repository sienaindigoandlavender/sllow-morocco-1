44 years, and the sultan was never deposed

A protectorate is not a colony, and the difference is not a technicality. The Moroccan state, the sultan, the courts and the ministries all continued to exist throughout, with French officials placed beside them. That is why independence in 1956 restored a country rather than inventing one.

One sultan, three administrations

The Treaty of Fes in 1912 put most of the country under French administration, a northern strip and a southern one under Spanish, and Tangier under an international committee with its own currency, courts and police. Three legal systems, and one throne above all of them.

The Tangier arrangement is why the writers arrived, and why Burroughs and the Bowleses could live as they did. A city run by committee governs lightly.

Forty-four years

From the Treaty of Fes to independence is less than half a century. A Moroccan born under the last independent sultan could have died in an independent kingdom having lived through the whole thing as an adult.

Lyautey’s doctrine, described in the Casablanca module, applied to more than architecture. The instruction was to govern through existing institutions and to leave the visible fabric of the country in place. It is the reason so much of pre-1912 Morocco is still standing and still functioning.

How independence actually came

MomentWhat happened
January 1944The Independence Manifesto. The Istiqlal party sets out the demand in writing, and the sultan does not disown it
August 1953Mohammed V is removed and exiled, to Corsica and then Madagascar. A replacement is installed
1953–1955The exile makes him the national symbol. Resistance widens, and the substitute has no authority
November 1955He returns. The crowds that meet him settle the question
2 March 1956France recognises independence. Spain follows for the northern zone in April

The exile is the decisive miscalculation. Removing the sultan was intended to break the independence movement, and instead it gave the movement a single figure to demand back. Two years later he returned, and the protectorate ended within four months.

Which is why 1956 is the date and not the whole story. Reassembling the country took another thirteen years, and the seams are still visible in the north and the far south: Spanish street names, a different café culture, and Spanish still spoken in Tetouan and Sidi Ifni.
