Tbourida, or Fantasia

Fifteen horses gallop two hundred metres in a line and fire at the same instant. If the crowd hears two cracks instead of one, the sorba has lost. Moroccans call it tbourida, after the word for gunpowder. The French called it fantasia, and that is the name most visitors arrive with.

Updated 7 September 2026 UNESCO inscription 2021. Founding dates are disputed; see below.

The formation

The mqadem rides at the centre and sets the pace. The line must stay level from the first stride to the last, accelerate together, and discharge as one. Riders carry a musket, a sword and a copy of the Quran, and wear the djellaba of their own region.

The horse under all of it

The Barb is among the oldest breeds in the world and one of the most widely used as foundation stock. Its descendants are better known than it is.

Barb
North African. Hard-hooved, short-backed, bred for the ground it came from
Andalusian
Iberia. The Barb crossed the strait with the armies and stayed
Lipizzaner
Austria. The Spanish Riding School horse, by way of Iberian stock
Thoroughbred
England. Most modern racing lines descend from three imported stallions, of which the Godolphin is generally held to have been a Barb

Eclipse, the horse behind a large share of the world’s racing pedigrees, traces back to that stallion. The breed’s origins are argued over and the Godolphin’s in particular, with accounts placing him variously as a Barb from Tunisia and as an Arabian from Yemen. The Barb attribution is the older and the more common one.

The manoeuvre is older than the gun. Riders were performing massed charges as displays of force long before there was anything to fire, and the shot was added once powder arrived. Which explains the spread of dates: they are dating different things.

Regional styles are distinct enough that an experienced eye can place a sorba by the bridles and the stitching on the riders’ belts. Women compete now, in their own sorbas, and the national trophy is held at Dar Es Salam.
