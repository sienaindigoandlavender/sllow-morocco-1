Morocco grows one olive

Picholine Marocaine covers more than 700,000 hectares and accounts for roughly 96% of national production. No other producing country depends on a single cultivar to anything like that degree.

One variety, almost everything

Spain works with dozens of cultivars, Italy with regional varieties by the hundred. Moroccan groves are effectively a monoculture, which makes the national flavour profile unusually consistent and the crop unusually exposed.

The same tree, two countries

Picholine Marocaine and the French Picholine du Languedoc trace back to the same olives in the Gard, in southern France. Centuries on either side of the Mediterranean pulled them apart: the French tree stayed a table olive, the Moroccan one became a dual-purpose workhorse bred for drought.
