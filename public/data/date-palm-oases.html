Eleven offshoots left in the 1930s

A fungus was killing the Mejhoul palms of the Draa. An American plant scientist was permitted to take eleven young shoots to California. Every Medjool date sold anywhere in the world descends from those eleven, and Morocco now imports them.

Compiled 7 September 2026 Historical. Palm-loss totals are estimates and vary between studies.

What left

Walter Swingle carried them out with the agreement of the French authorities, who had failed to stop the disease by any other means. The name lost its shape on the crossing: Mejhoul became Medjool.

Where they went, and where they sell

From the Coachella Valley the variety was carried to the Jordan Valley, and from both places it is exported to Saudi Arabia, North Africa and Morocco itself.

What the fungus took

Bayoud is Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. albedinis, a soil fungus that blocks the palm’s vascular system. It was first reported in Morocco in 1870 and has been working outward ever since.

Roughly two thirds of Morocco’s best date varieties are gone. The fungus attacks the good cultivars hardest: Mejhoul, Boufeggous and Deglet Nour are all highly susceptible, and the resistant palms tend to make poor dates. The European Union treats it as a quarantine pathogen.

The sequence

1870
Bayoud is first reported
In Morocco. It spreads from here into Algeria and Mauritania.
1927
The Mejhoul groves fail
The French colonial administration convenes scientists from several countries. None of them can stop it.
1930s
Eleven offshoots to California
Walter Swingle takes them to the Coachella Valley. They take.
1981
The pathogen is isolated at Rissani
From a Mejhoul palm in the Tafilalet. That isolate is still the reference strain.
2000s
Najda
INRA breeds a resistant variety at the Ternata oasis station in the Middle Draa, combining resistance with an edible date.
2011–13
Replanting begins
Mostly Najda, under the Green Morocco Plan.
2015–
Mejhoul comes home
New plantings favour Mejhoul and Boufeggous over the older varieties. The premium cultivars are still only about a fifth of national supply.

Why a palm cannot simply be replanted

ProblemDetail
Seed does not breed trueThe date palm is dioecious with 36 chromosomes, so seedlings come out variable. The mixed populations have their own name: khalt
Offshoots are the only copyA named variety is propagated from shoots at the base of the parent, which is why eleven of them could carry a cultivar across an ocean
The fungus lives in the soilIt persists for years without a host, so replanting the same ground with the same cultivar fails again
Resistance and quality pull apartPalms that survive Bayoud have historically made indifferent dates. Najda was bred to hold both
