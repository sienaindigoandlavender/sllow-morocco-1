300 guests is modest

A Moroccan wedding runs a live orchestra, five to seven bridal outfits, a professional who choreographs the whole appearance, and a feast for several hundred people. It is one of the largest craft economies in the country, paid for a household at a time.

The negafa is the central figure: a professional who supplies the caftans and takchitas, the jewellery, the amariya, the makeup, and the choreography of the procession, and who manages the bride through every change of the night.

Measured against a wage

The median monthly wage in Morocco is around 3,500 dirhams. Each block below is one month of it. A 200,000 dirham wedding is the gold one at the end of that row.

57 months of median earnings, spent on a single evening.

Families save for years toward it and some borrow. The scale of the expectation is one of the reasons the average age of marriage in Morocco keeps rising.

The evening, in order

Afternoon
The bride goes to the negafa. Hair, makeup, the first of the outfits, the jewellery laid out.
9pm
Guests arrive. Dried fruit and pastries, tea and juice, the orchestra already playing.
11pm
The bride enters on the amariya, carried in, usually in a white takchita with a wide belt.
Through the night
Four to six further changes, each a different region, era or colour. The walima is served: pastilla, mechoui, tagine, couscous, then fruit, pastries and tea.
Dawn
The last set of the orchestra. Weddings end when they end.

Who gets paid

The wedding is where several of the crafts in this archive actually earn their living.

TradeWhat the wedding buys
EmbroiderersOne to three months of hand work per outfit, five to seven outfits deep
Caftan makers and belt makersThe belt is treated as the piece that carries the whole look
JewellersIncluding the necklace called the thread of the soul, considered essential for the henna night
MusiciansChaabi or Andalusi orchestras, live, for hours. Families compete on the quality of the band

Traditionally the groom’s side carried most of the cost, including the bride’s outfits and jewellery on top of the sadaq. Splitting the bill between the two families is now common.
