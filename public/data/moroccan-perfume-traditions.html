4 tonnes of petals, 1 kilogram of oil

Rose oil is the most concentrated agricultural product Morocco makes. Four thousand kilograms of damask petals, picked before the sun is up over six weeks in one valley, reduce in a copper still to about a kilogram of liquid. Everything else that comes out is rose water.

Updated 7 September 2026 Yields vary with season, variety and still. The ratio is the standard order of magnitude.

The still

Petals and water together over a fire. The steam carries the volatile compounds up the swan neck, a cold coil condenses it, and what collects in the separator settles into two layers: a thin film of essential oil on top and litres of hydrosol underneath.

The hydrosol is not a by-product in Morocco. Rose water is used in cooking, in the hammam, on guests’ hands before a meal, and at funerals. The oil goes to Grasse. The water stays.

Every flower. There is no mechanical alternative

Rosa damascena is grown at Kelaa M’Gouna in the Dades, in hedges along the field terraces rather than as a crop in its own right. The hedges are a windbreak and a boundary marker that happen to be worth more than what they enclose.

Where each scent comes from

Neroli from the bitter orange groves around Meknes and in the Souss. Damask rose in the Dades. Saffron at Taliouine. Thuya wood at Essaouira. Mint everywhere, and best from Meknes. The perfumers are in Fes and Marrakech, which is to say at the end of the roads rather than at the source.

Three ways to get the smell out

MethodUsed for
Steam distillationRose, neroli, lavender, rosemary, thyme. Anything whose compounds survive being boiled
Cold pressingCitrus peel. The oil sits in cells just under the skin and is squeezed out
Enfleurage and macerationJasmine and orange blossom, which are destroyed by heat. Petals laid in fat or oil until it takes the scent

Neroli is the interesting case. The bitter orange gives three different materials from three different parts: neroli from the blossom by distillation, petitgrain from the leaves and twigs, and bitter orange oil from the peel by pressing. One tree, three products, three techniques.

What is worn, and how

FormPractice
Musk and oilApplied neat to the skin at the pulse points, in small quantity, and not sprayed
Rose waterPoured over the hands from a long-necked flask before and after eating
BakhourSmoke taken into clothes and hair, described at length elsewhere in this archive
Ghassoul and the hammamClay washed with rose or orange blossom water. The scent is on the skin before anything is applied to it

Which is the difference from a European scent culture. Perfume here is not a single bottle sprayed at the neck in the morning. It is layered through washing, dressing, eating and receiving, in four or five different forms, all through the day.
