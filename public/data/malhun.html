Poetry written by coppersmiths

Malhun came out of the craft guilds. Tanners, weavers, coppersmiths and saddlers wrote it, in Darija rather than classical Arabic, and set it to the modes of the court music they were not invited to. UNESCO listed it in December 2023.

Updated 7 September 2026 Origin dates range across five centuries in the literature; the range is given below.

How a qasida is built

An instrumental overture sets the mode. Then the alternation begins: a verse sung alone, then the harba taken up by everyone — the word means launch, and the form goes back to the sixteenth century. Solo, all, solo, all, for as long as the poem lasts, which can be more than an hour.

That structure is the class marker. A nuba is performed at an audience. A malhun qasida requires the room to answer.

Malhun borrows its modes from al-Ala. Same tonal system, same family of instruments — oud, violin, rebab and small drums — and a completely different social origin. The craftsmen took the court’s music and put their own language in it.

What it is about

Tawassul
The mystical plea. Sufi in character, and often used to open a performance
Ghazal
Love and beauty, worked through recurring figures: the candle, the messenger
The feast
Whole qasidas given over to describing a meal, dish by dish and ingredient by ingredient
The political
Social commentary, and resistance. It has been used that way throughout its history
The imaginary journey
Travel to places the poet had not been
Nature and the city
Gardens, seasons, weather, and the medina the poet worked in

The gastronomic qasidas are the ones nobody expects. A poem that lists a feast in order is, incidentally, a dated record of what was cooked and what went into it — a recipe archive that survived because it rhymed.

How old it is, and the honest answer

Three datings circulate. Some scholarship places the origin in the twelfth century under the Almohads. The common account gives the fifteenth. Others put the mature workshop form in the seventeenth, in Meknes and Fes.

They are probably dating different things: the poetic form, its combination with music, and its consolidation inside the guilds. Ibn Khaldun refers to a related vernacular genre at the end of the Muqaddimah, which is the earliest anchor anyone has.

The UNESCO file makes a point of saying malhun is performed and enjoyed by people of all genders now. It did not begin that way: the guilds were closed to women, and so was the form that came out of them.
