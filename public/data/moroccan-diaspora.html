One Moroccan in six lives somewhere else

Six point two million people, across more than a hundred countries, sending home more money than the phosphate industry earns. Officially they are Marocains Résidant à l’Étranger. Everyone calls them MRE.

Compiled 7 September 2026 Latest figures: 2025 remittances and the Q4 2026 diaspora observatory.

The road north

The dashed line is the route most of the first generation took: out of the Rif through Nador, across to Spain, north to France, and on to Belgium and the Netherlands. Every summer it runs in reverse through Tangier Med and Nador.

Moroccans are the largest immigrant group in Spain. In the Netherlands they are 2.4% of the population, concentrated in the Randstad: Amsterdam, Rotterdam, The Hague and Utrecht account for most of them.

The money going the other way

Remittances are the quietest large number in the Moroccan economy. They pay for the half-finished concrete houses you see all over the countryside, and they arrive whether or not the harvest did.

Remittances, 2025
$12.4 bn
Up from $11.7 bn in 2024, which was more than 8% of GDP. Among the largest inflows in the region.
Tourism receipts, 2025
$13.5 bn
Tourism has now edged ahead. For most of the past decade the diaspora sent home more than visitors spent.

The Rif in Rotterdam

The Dutch Moroccan community is not, in the main, Arabic-speaking. It came overwhelmingly from the Rif, and it kept the language.

Language spoken at home, NetherlandsShare
Amazigh, principally Tarifit86%
Moroccan Arabic14%

Which means the largest Amazigh-speaking population outside North Africa sits in a handful of Dutch and Belgian cities, and that a second-generation Riffian in Rotterdam may speak Tarifit and Dutch without ever having needed Arabic.
