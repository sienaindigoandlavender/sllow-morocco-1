The mountains are too high for their roots

A range this tall should sit on a thick crustal root, the way an iceberg sits on the mass below it. The High Atlas does not have one. The crust here has barely been shortened, the lithosphere is at its thinnest exactly where the peaks are highest, and what is holding Toubkal up appears to be heat.

Updated 7 September 2026 Geological. The mantle-support model is current interpretation, not settled fact.

2 ways to hold a mountain up

Crustal shortening is what builds a root, and at the Tizi n’Tichka pass the western High Atlas has been shortened by about thirteen kilometres, roughly 15%. That is not much. Further east there is more shortening and the mountains are lower, which is the opposite of what a root model predicts. The reading now favoured is that hot mantle beneath a thinned lithosphere is buoying the range from below.

South to north, and the age underneath

The South Atlas Fault is the line in oxide. South of it lies the Anti-Atlas, sitting on the West African Craton: Precambrian basement, schist, granite and mylonite. North of it the High Atlas is a fold and thrust belt of Palaeozoic and Mesozoic rock over the same old basement. Crossing that fault takes you back a billion years in a few kilometres.

The Anti-Atlas and the Appalachians are the same event

Around 300 million years ago Africa collided with North America and Pangaea closed. The mountains thrown up along that suture are believed to include both the Anti-Atlas and the Appalachians, and the chain is thought to have rivalled the Himalayas at the time.

Then the Atlantic opened and split it. What is left in Morocco is the worn-down southern stump of a range whose other half is in Pennsylvania. The ophiolite slivers at Bou Azzer, Siroua and Iriri are older still: fragments of ocean floor, left behind when an earlier ocean closed.

3 phases, not one

WhenWhat happened
c. 300 million years agoAfrica meets North America. The Anti-Atlas is raised. Pangaea assembles
MesozoicPangaea tears apart. The crust here stretches instead of compressing, and rift basins fill with thick sediment
Cenozoic to nowAfrica converges on Eurasia. The old rift is inverted and pushed back up as the High Atlas. The same convergence built the Alps and the Pyrenees and narrowed Gibraltar

Which is why the range reads as one thing and behaves as three. The material was laid down in a rift, the structure came from a collision, and the elevation appears to come from the mantle.
