The capital has moved fourteen times

Morocco has four imperial cities because it never settled on one. Power shifted between them for twelve hundred years, and Fes alone lost it and got it back six separate times.

Compiled 7 September 2026 Historical. Several handover dates are disputed by a year or two.

Twelve centuries, four cities

Each band is one continuous stint as capital. The gaps in the sequence are the interregnums, and the narrow slivers are the reigns that lasted four or six years.

Fes has been the capital for roughly two thirds of the time there has been a Morocco to have a capital of. Meknes managed fifty-five years, all of them under one man.

Who moved it, and why

DynastyCapital, and the reason
Idrisid, from 789Fes. Inland and defensible, in the fertile Saïs, away from both Byzantine reach on the coast and the tribal south
Almoravid, 1071Marrakech. A new city on the plain below the Atlas, close to the caravan routes their empire ran on
Almohad, 1147Marrakech, inherited and rebuilt. They also fortified Rabat as a launching point for Iberia
Marinid, 1244Fes again, with a whole new walled city, Fes el Jdid, built beside the old one
Saadi, 1554Marrakech, funded by Saharan gold and the sugar trade
Alaouite, 1672Meknes, because Moulay Ismail wanted a capital of his own making
Alaouite, 1727Fes, once Moulay Ismail was dead and the building stopped
Protectorate, 1912Rabat. The French moved it to the coast, for the port and the ships. It stayed there at independence

North and south

The pattern underneath the dates is geographic. Dynasties that came out of the Sahara ruled from Marrakech; dynasties that looked to the Mediterranean and to Andalusia ruled from Fes. The capital moved when the centre of gravity did.

What each city was left with

CityWhat the imperial period built
FesAl-Qarawiyyin, the Marinid madrasas, the largest surviving medieval medina anywhere
MarrakechThe Koutoubia, the Saadian Tombs, El Badi, the Agdal and Menara gardens
MeknesBab Mansour, the granaries, stabling built for twelve thousand horses
RabatThe unfinished Hassan Tower, the Kasbah of the Udayas, Chellah on a Roman site

All four medinas are UNESCO listed. The rivalry is why: each dynasty built to outdo the last one, in a different city, and none of them tore down what came before.
