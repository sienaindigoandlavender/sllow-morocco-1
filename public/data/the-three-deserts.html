Most of the Sahara is not sand

An erg is a sea of dunes. A hamada is stone. Morocco has a great deal of the second and comparatively little of the first, and the two ergs that everyone photographs cover a small fraction of the country’s dry land.

Agafay lies inside the Marrakech plain. The Sahara proper begins on the far side of the Atlas, several hundred kilometres south of it.

Where they are

The two ergs sit six hours apart and drain different river systems. The Ziz feeds the sediment at Chebbi; the Draa feeds Chigaga, and stops just short of it.

Erg Chigaga fares worse: maximum dune height is given as 120 metres in some accounts and 300 in others. Dunes also move, which is a defence nobody making these claims has offered.

The words worth knowing

TermWhat it means
ErgA sand sea. Dunes formed and moved by wind, deep enough to bury everything under them
HamadaStone desert. Bare rock and gravel, swept clear of sand. Most of the Sahara looks like this
RegA gravel plain, the surface between the two
OuedA watercourse, dry most of the year and dangerous when it is not
PalmeraieThe irrigated palm belt along a river, where anyone actually lives
