1 fire, 3 rooms, and a queue of other people’s dinners

A hammam is a heat engine. Wood burns at the back all day, the gas runs under the floors through a Roman hypocaust, and the rooms get cooler the further you walk from the furnace. The same fire heats the bread and cooks the tanjia.

Updated 7 September 2026 Prices are 2026 figures from Marrakech neighbourhood baths.

Section through a neighbourhood bath

The layout is Roman and the ritual is Islamic. Rome built the caldarium, tepidarium and frigidarium, and the hypocaust that heats them; the Islamic world kept the machinery and changed what it was for, from a civic leisure building to a requirement of ritual purity.

The sequence

The order is not decorative. Each stage makes the next one work, and skipping the soap halves the result of the scrub.

01
Sit in the hot room
Heat opens the pores and softens the outer layer of skin. Vasodilation, in a word, and the reason everything after this works.
10–20 min
02
Savon beldi
Black soap, made from fermented olive pulp, often with eucalyptus. Buttery, dark, faintly earthy. Spread on and left to sit while the olive compounds loosen the dead layer.
5–10 min
03
Kessa
A coarse woven mitt, worked in long strokes from the feet upward. Grey rolls come off. This is the part everybody talks about afterwards.
15 min
04
Ghassoul
Mineral clay from the Atlas, on skin and hair. Magnesium, calcium, silica. Optional, and common.
10 min
05
Cold rinse
Ends the heat, closes the pores, and is the single most reliable moment of the whole thing.
—
06
Argan oil
Applied to freshly cleared skin, which is when it absorbs best.
—

What it costs

A neighbourhood hammam and a hotel hammam are the same ritual at two different prices. The public bath expects you to bring your own kit.

At a public bathPrice
Entry10 MAD
Scrub by an attendant, the tayyaba or kessala50 MAD
Bring yourselfkessa, black soap, shampoo, sandals, towel, a stool

Most Moroccans go once a week, and almost all of them have a bathroom at home. That is the part visitors misread: it is not a substitute for plumbing.

The furnace has 3 jobs

The man who feeds the fire is the farnatchi, and the heat he makes does not only warm the bath.

What the fire doesHow
Heats the waterA cauldron over the flame, and steam sent through channels into the hot room
Heats the floorsHot gas under the raised floor of the hot and warm rooms, then up the flues in the walls
Bakes and cooksThe bread oven often shares the fire or the wall. Tanjia urns go down into the ash pit beside it and come out at the end of the day

Nothing in the arrangement wastes heat, which is the reason the hammam, the ferran and the mosque tend to sit within a few doors of each other. They are one piece of infrastructure with three entrances.
