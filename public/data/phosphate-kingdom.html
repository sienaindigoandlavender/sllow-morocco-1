Fifty billion tonnes

Morocco holds the world's phosphate. Estimates of the share run from 68% to three quarters depending on who is counting, and every fertiliser bag in Brazil, India and Europe traces back to it.

Compiled 7 September 2026 Latest figures: 2025 production and OCP half-year results.

Reserves, in billions of tonnes

Global reserves stand at roughly 74 billion tonnes. Morocco holds 50 billion of them. The United States, which mines a fifth as much rock every year, has one.

Holding it and mining it are different things

China mines more phosphate than anyone and is running down a far smaller stock to do it. Morocco is second on annual output and first on what is left in the ground, which is the position that matters over a century.

Where it comes out of the ground

Mining is open-pit, which is most of the cost advantage. Khouribga and Gantour feed the two chemical hubs; the rock leaves through four ports. At Bou Craa the rock travels to the coast on a conveyor belt around a hundred kilometres long, long enough to be visible from orbit.

A Moroccan rock in a field in the Punjab

Phosphate has no substitute. Nothing else feeds a plant the way it does, and there is no synthetic route to it. Which means that half the wheat grown in India and a large share of the soy in Brazil depends on rock cut out of the ground at Khouribga.

When Russia's invasion of Ukraine disrupted fertiliser supply in 2022, Moroccan phosphate exports rose 54.8% in a year and brought in close to €10 billion. A commodity nobody thinks about became a lever nobody could ignore.
