12,000 trucks and 112 trains

The Green March moved 350,000 civilians to the edge of the Sahara in three weeks, fed them, watered them and brought them home. Set aside what it was for and it remains the largest civilian logistics operation in Moroccan history.

Updated 7 September 2026 This module covers the organisation of the march. The territorial question is outside its scope.

How you organise a third of a million people

By breaking them into units small enough for one person to be responsible for, and stacking the responsibility. The structure came from the way tribes and provinces already worked.

What had to be carried

Tarfaya is a small town on the Atlantic edge of the desert. Nothing about it could support the population of a city arriving at once.

Marrakech was designated the temporary seat of government for the duration, and volunteers were routed through it before going south to Tarfaya.

The dates

16 Oct 1975
The International Court of Justice issues its advisory opinion. Hassan II announces the march the same day, by radio and television.
31 Oct
Volunteers continue gathering at Tarfaya.
5 Nov
The King addresses the assembled marchers on the eve of the crossing.
6 Nov
The march crosses. Spanish forces are under orders not to fire. Accounts of the distance covered range from ten to forty kilometres.
9 Nov
The march is halted and the return begins.
14 Nov
The Madrid Accords are signed.
18 Nov
Complete withdrawal of the civilian marchers is ordered.
