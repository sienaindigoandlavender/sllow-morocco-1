The cross is a man and the diamond is a woman

The two commonest marks on a Beni Ourain are a gendered pair, and the field reads differently once you know which is which.

Compiled 7 September 2026 Symbol readings vary between tribes and are not a fixed alphabet.

Two marks

The cross
Masculine. It stands for the metalworker, who is held in unusual regard because iron turns away harm. A field of them is a field of protection.
The lozenge
Feminine. Fertility, and by extension the household. Weaving is women’s work, so the diamond is the weaver signing her own field.

Different confederations use the same shapes differently, so a dealer offering a single fixed dictionary is going further than the evidence does.

The colours came out of the ground

Before aniline dyes arrived, every colour in a Moroccan carpet had a source you could name and mostly grow.

Which is why abrash matters. When the dye lot ran out and the next batch came up slightly different, a band of the carpet changes colour partway up. Machines do not do that. A sudden shift across the field is the strongest single sign that a rug was dyed in small quantities by hand.

Not one thing

Beni Ourain is a confederation of seventeen tribes, not a style. Azilal is a province. Boucherouite is not a place at all: the word comes from the Arabic for a scrap of torn cloth.

NameWhat it is
Beni OurainKnotted pile, undyed cream wool, black or brown geometry. Made as bedding against Middle Atlas winters, which is why the pile is long
AzilalKnotted pile, cream ground, dyed wool in free composition. Themes are marriage, birth, farming. No two alike
BoujadPink, violet and red, looser drawing, from the plains west of the Middle Atlas
Beni MrirtFiner knot, denser pile, the technically accomplished cousin of the Beni Ourain
GlaouiNamed for the Ouarzazate chieftains. Alternating bands of flat weave and pile in one piece
Kilim, or hanbelFlat woven rather than knotted. No pile at all
BoucherouiteKnotted from cut-up clothing and fabric scraps. Born of not having wool
RabatUrban, high knot count, Ottoman-influenced medallions. Carries a Ministry of Handicrafts label

Where they are woven

The wool differs before the weaver touches it. Sheep in the Beni Ourain country carry very white fleece; Azilal wool runs beige. The palette starts with the pasture.

How a domestic object became a design object

Beni Ourain rugs were made to sleep under. They were long and narrow because a body is, and thick because the Middle Atlas freezes.

Mid-century modernist architects found them in the 1950s and put them in rooms of glass and steel, where the undyed wool and the flat geometry sat well against Bauhaus furniture, and the same object acquired a second market without altering in any respect. Nearly every Beni Ourain sold internationally today goes onto a floor with central heating under it.
