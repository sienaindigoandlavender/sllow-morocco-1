320 km/h, and 357 on test

Africa’s first high-speed railway runs 323 kilometres from Tangier to Casablanca in two hours and ten minutes, on 186 kilometres of purpose-built line and an upgraded conventional alignment for the rest. It is still the only one on the continent.

Updated 7 September 2026 Latest: the Kenitra–Marrakech works launched April 2025.

The line, by design speed

Tangier to Kenitra is purpose-built for 320 km/h. Kenitra to Casablanca is the old alignment with a third track added, rated at 160 and being taken to 220, which is a cheaper way to buy most of the time saving. The extension to Marrakech was launched in April 2025 and is due before the end of 2029.

The third bar is the one worth looking at. Tangier to Marrakech is 580 kilometres, and the plan is to cover it in half an hour more than Casablanca takes today.

Published targets disagree. The 2040 Rail Strategy gives 1,100 km, ONCF documents have used 1,280 km by 2040 and 1,500 km by 2035, and the total length of the conventional network is variously given as 2,100 and 4,225 kilometres depending on what is counted as a line.
