Most of them were never riads

Agents count about eleven thousand riads inside the walls of the Marrakech medina. The number is really a count of houses. The riad proper is a garden house and it belongs to a few wealthy quarters; the rest of the medina was built for artisans and workers, in dars, and a dar becomes a riad when somebody buys it and converts it.

The buyers are principally French, British, Belgian, Dutch, Swiss and American. Across Marrakech as a whole, foreign buyers and Moroccans living abroad account for something like 35 to 40% of all acquisitions; inside the medina the concentration is roughly double that.

The difference is the garden

Riad is the Arabic for gardens. A riad is a house built around a planted courtyard, classically four beds with a tree in each and water where the paths cross. A dar has a houch: an open paved yard, no planting, rooms packed tight around it to the shape of whatever plot was available.

Which quarter you are standing in

The distinction is geographic before it is architectural. Garden houses need land and water, so they were built where the money was.

QuarterWhat was built there
Ksour, Mouassine, Dar el BachaThe palace quarters, near the royal complex. Riads and dars of real size, with gardens, multiple patios and carved cedar ceilings
Derb Dabachi, Riad Zitoun, Bab Doukkala and most of the restArtisans, traders and working households. Dars and douirat, built small and close, without gardens

Which means the eleven thousand is a housing stock figure. The number of houses that were built as riads is a fraction of it, and concentrated in a few derbs.

How a dar becomes a riad

The word is applied backwards by the market. A working house is bought, two or three of them are sometimes knocked together, the houch is planted or a plunge pool is sunk into it, a fountain goes in, and the property is listed as a riad. The garden arrives with the buyer.

BeforeAfter
Dar with a paved houch, one family, rooms off the yardListed and sold as a riad
Adjoining dars in separate ownershipKnocked through into one property with the room count a guest house needs

Storage and service rooms at ground levelReception, kitchen, salon

Much of it has kept buildings standing that were falling down, under a permit regime that requires traditional materials. It does mean that counting riads in the medina counts an outcome rather than a building type.

What restoration involves

The medina is a UNESCO-classified zone, so structural work is not a private matter.

RequirementDetail
AuthorisationEvery structural intervention requires approval from the Inspection des Monuments Historiques
TimescaleThree to six months minimum for permits, before any work starts
TradesSpecialist craftsmen for zellige, tadelakt and carved cedar. The apprenticeships behind those are eight to ten years
Acquisition costs8 to 10% on top of price: about 4% registration, 1.5 to 2% notary, 0.5 to 1% land registry
AccessDistance from vehicle access is a material factor. A house at the end of a lane no cart can reach is a different proposition from one five minutes from a car

That permit regime is why the conversions look the way they do. It is slow, it is expensive, and it is also the reason the fabric has been repaired in traditional materials rather than concrete.

What the pipeline says about 2030

Marrakech is a host city for the 2030 World Cup, and around 3,000 premium rooms are reported to be in the pipeline against it. Transactions across the city rose roughly 12% between 2024 and 2026, at annual volumes of about eighteen thousand.

Which sets up the question the medina will answer over the next few years, and this module does not attempt to answer it: how many of the remaining nine and a half thousand stay lived in.
