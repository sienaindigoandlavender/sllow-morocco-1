2,000 km of open water, and a coast facing straight into it

Morocco’s Atlantic shore points north-west at the exact quarter where winter storms form, with nothing in between. The swell that arrives has had two thousand kilometres to organise itself, and then it meets a coastline made almost entirely of headlands.

Updated 7 September 2026 Physical description. Conditions vary daily and this is not a surf forecast.

Where the swell comes from

Low pressure systems form off Newfoundland and Greenland and track east. The distance a wind blows over open water is called fetch, and fetch is what turns chop into swell: the longer the run, the more the waves sort themselves into long, even lines travelling at the same speed.

By the time that swell reaches Morocco it has crossed an ocean with no island chain and no continental shelf to break it up. It arrives clean, which is a different thing from arriving big.

Why the waves here are long

A wave slows down when it starts to feel the bottom. Approach a headland at an angle and the end nearest the shallows slows first, so the line bends and begins breaking from one end. That is a point break: it peels in one direction, at walking pace, for as far as the reef runs.

A beach break has no such geometry. The sandbanks move, the wave breaks all at once, and the ride is over in seconds. Morocco’s coast has an unusual number of the first kind, because the Atlas reaches the sea in a long series of rocky points.

How the fishing village became a surf town

Taghazout was a working fishing village that Europeans began arriving at in the late sixties, overland, in vans. What they had found was a concentration of world-class right-hand points within a few kilometres of each other, in warm air, cheap, and reachable by road from Europe.

The road is the underrated part. Morocco is the only place with waves of that quality that a European can drive to, which is why the surf population here is measured in generations rather than seasons, and why most of the camps are now run by Moroccans who grew up in the water.
