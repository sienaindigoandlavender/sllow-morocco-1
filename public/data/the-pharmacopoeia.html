743 plants in the pharmacopoeia

Moroccan universities have spent two decades surveying what the attarine actually sell and what households actually use, region by region, with citation indices and statistical consensus measures. The result is not folklore. It is a documented pharmacopoeia of 743 taxa across 101 families.

Updated 7 September 2026 Compiled from published ethnobotanical surveys, 2019–2025.

From the flora to the pharmacopoeia

743 taxa, in 101 families and 371 genera. Four families carry most of it: Asteraceae at 10.9%, Lamiaceae at 10.8%, Fabaceae at 5.9% and Apiaceae at 5.1%. The Lamiaceae, which is the mint and thyme family, holds about half of everything endemic.

Digestive complaints account for about 40% of everything treated. In the Marrakech survey, 86 plants were recorded against 26 named conditions, in that case with Asteraceae leading, then Apiaceae and Lamiaceae.

Studied properly, and by whom

These surveys use the standard quantitative apparatus of the field: Relative Frequency of Citation, Use Value, Fidelity Level, Informant Consensus Factor, Family Importance Value. Which means the results are comparable between regions and testable against each other.

Work of this kind is also producing new records. The Taza study reported four species not previously documented for that use anywhere in the literature, which is what a live research field looks like.

The part the souk stall does not say

This module documents practice. It is not medical guidance. Several plants in regular medicinal use in Morocco are toxic, and the central Middle Atlas has its own published inventory of them. Dose, preparation and identification all matter, an attarine is not a pharmacist, and the surveys record what people do rather than what works. Nothing here should be used to treat anything.
