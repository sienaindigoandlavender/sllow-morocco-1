It is the largest car-free urban area on earth. Goods still move by donkey and handcart because most of the lanes are too narrow for anything else, which is also why satellite mapping represents it so badly.

The trades are arranged by distance from the mosque

A medina is not a random tangle. Proximity to the Friday mosque is the value gradient, and the trades sort themselves along it: clean and quiet at the centre, loud and dirty at the walls where the water leaves.

Which is why the perfumers' souk sits against Al-Qarawiyyin and the tanneries are pushed out to the river. The arrangement is a thousand years old and was enforced by rent.

The quarter is the unit, not the street

A medina is an assembly of self-contained neighbourhoods. Each historically carried the same five institutions, and a quarter that had all five could function without reference to the rest of the city.

Mosque
Prayer, and the call that divides the day
Hammam
Washing, and the furnace that heats it
Ferran
The communal oven. Most houses have none
Fountain
Water, before it was piped
Msid
The Quranic school

The hammam furnace and the bread oven are often the same fire, or share a wall. Nothing here was designed to waste heat.
