Seksu

The word is Amazigh, and steamers for it have been recovered from tombs of the third century BCE. Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia and Mauritania have each claimed the dish, and in 2020 they gave up and filed for it together.

Updated 7 September 2026 Historical, with the inscription record from UNESCO 15.COM, 2020.

Libya eats it and did not sign. The inscription covers the knowledge and the practices rather than the dish, which is what allowed four states with real disagreements to put their names on one document.

Older than every state that claimed it

3rd c. BCE
Steamers in Amazigh tombs
Keskas vessels have been recovered from burials of this date. The word seksu is Amazigh, and the dish is older than Arabic in North Africa by roughly nine centuries.
7th c.
The Arab conquest
Couscous is already here. What arrives is a new language for it and eventually a new week to eat it in.
Early 20th c.
Europe learns the word
French colonialism and the Pieds-Noirs of Algeria carry it to French tables, and from there to the rest of Europe.
2020
Inscribed jointly
UNESCO’s fifteenth committee session accepts the four-country file. The Director-General calls it a diplomatic result as much as a cultural one.

3 steamings

The couscoussier is a two-part pot: broth simmering below, grain steaming above in a perforated basket. Nothing boils. The grain is cooked entirely by vapour rising from the stew it will be served with.

The packet in a British supermarket takes three minutes and boiling water. It is the same grain and a different food.

Who the inscription actually names

UNESCO listed a chain of people, not a recipe: the farmers who grow the cereal, the millers who turn it into semolina, the artisans who make the couscoussier and the wooden bowls, the traders, and the women who hold the knowledge of the rolling and the steaming and pass it on.

Which is why the file could be filed jointly. No one country can claim a chain that runs across all of them.
