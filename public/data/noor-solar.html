Noor Ouarzazate runs after dark

Two million mirrors on six thousand acres of hammada, storing the day’s heat in molten salt and generating for three hours after the sun has gone. It is the largest concentrated solar complex in the world, and China sent a delegation to Morocco to study how it works.

Compiled 7 September 2026 Capacity and cost figures from the project record; targets from the Moroccan Solar Plan.

Why storage is the whole point

Photovoltaic panels convert light directly and stop when it goes. A concentrated plant makes heat instead, holds it in molten salt, and can turn that heat into steam whenever the grid asks for it. Peak household demand in Morocco arrives in the evening, which is exactly when a PV farm has nothing left to give.

Why China came to look

China builds more photovoltaic capacity than the United States, Japan and Europe combined, and manufactures most of the world’s panels. Concentrated solar with thermal storage is a different discipline, and a thirty-member Chinese delegation came to Ouarzazate to study it. More than a hundred officials and utility managers from seven countries across the Middle East and North Africa followed.

The International Energy Agency has estimated that concentrated solar could supply up to 11% of world electricity by 2050. The working reference plant is in southern Morocco.
