12 months, and almost nothing overlaps

Roses for six weeks in May. Saffron for six weeks in October. Dates in September, olives from October, citrus all winter. A Moroccan market is a calendar, and most of what is in it this week will be gone by next month.

Updated 7 September 2026 Windows shift by a fortnight or so with altitude and with the year.

Why the windows are so narrow

Altitude does most of it. The same crop ripens weeks apart between the Souss plain and a valley at fifteen hundred metres, which is why a market in Marrakech and one in Azilal can be selling different things on the same Tuesday.

AltitudeRoughly a fortnight’s delay for every few hundred metres up

The three that stop the clock

CropWhat the window costs
Damask rose, Kelaa M’GounaSix weeks in April and May. Picked before the sun is up, because the oil goes with the heat
Saffron, TaliouineSix weeks in October and November. A hundred and fifty thousand flowers to the kilogram, all by hand
OlivesEight weeks from October. Pressed within days of picking, or the oil is worth less

All three are described at length elsewhere in this archive. What the wheel adds is that they do not overlap: the same valley can only give its whole attention to one of them at a time, and the labour moves round the country behind the harvest.
