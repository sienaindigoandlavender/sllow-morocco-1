The square that invented a category

Jemaa el-Fna was the first place on earth proclaimed a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity. The category itself came out of the campaign to protect it, on a Moroccan resolution, after a meeting held in Marrakech. Every intangible listing anywhere since runs through this square.

How a car park changed international law

1922
Protected as Moroccan artistic heritage
Seventy-nine years before UNESCO arrived, the square was already listed nationally.
1996
The plans
An underground car park and a fifteen-metre building beside the square. Juan Goytisolo, the Spanish writer who lived in Marrakech, raised the alarm.
1997
The International Consultation, held in Marrakech
On the Preservation of Popular Cultural Spaces. Out of it, the Moroccan authorities submitted a draft resolution proposing an international distinction for intangible heritage, which did not yet exist.
2001
Proclaimed, in the first round
Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, May 2001. The category was made for spaces like this one.
2003
The Convention
UNESCO adopts the Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage, the instrument the proclamations were the trial run for.
2008
Inscribed on the Representative List
The proclamations were folded into the new framework, and the square carried across.

The halqa

Halqa means circle. One performer holds the centre and the audience makes the walls, and the form is the reason the square could be listed at all: there is nothing to preserve here except people doing something in front of other people.

Who works the circles
Storytellers, working in Darija, and drawing the largest local crowds
Gnawa troupes, and Amazigh musicians
Acrobats and comedians
Herbalists, healers and seers
Snake handlers and animal men

What the name is understood to mean

Three readings circulate and none has been settled.

Assembly of the dead
For executions carried out here under the Almoravids and Almohads, with heads displayed at the perimeter.
Mosque of the dead
For a mosque nearby that was begun and never finished, or that partly collapsed in the eighteenth century.
Assembly of nothingness
Reading fna as emptiness: a space defined by having no fixed use, which is the one that best describes what it does.

The day the square keeps

Morning
Orange-juice carts set up. Herbalists lay out remedies. Animal handlers arrive. The square is at its quietest and easiest to cross.
Midday
Trade rather than performance. The souks behind take the crowd.
About 5pm
The food stalls are assembled from nothing, numbered and lit. The whole surface of the square changes use inside an hour.
After dark
The circles form. Music, storytelling, comedy, and the smoke off the grills.
Late
The stalls come down again and the square is empty by morning.

A square that is built and dismantled every day, and has been doing it since the eleventh century.
